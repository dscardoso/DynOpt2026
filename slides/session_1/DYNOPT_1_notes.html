1.15 Be careful with variable scope

Inside a file, a loop at the top level cannot assign to a variable defined outside it. It quietly makes a new one that dies with the loop, and the warning scrolls past

. . .

If the loop also reads the variable, total = total + i, the file version stops with UndefVarError instead. Same cause

. . .

To fix this, place every loop inside a function

Code 
function sum_to(n)
    total = 0.0
    for i in 1:n
        total = total + i
    end
    return total
end
sum_to(3)
6.0

2 2. One reservoir, one season

2.1 The setting

One reservoir. It holds s = 4 units of water this season

. . .

  • In spring, release x units for irrigation. Farmers receive F(x)
  • Over the summer the reservoir holds s - x. Recreational users receive U(s - x)
  • Then the season ends. There is no tomorrow (in the model). We’ll relax this next week

2.2 The model

\max_{0 \le x \le 4} \; F(x) + U(4 - x), \qquad F(x) = \alpha_1 x^{\beta_1}, \quad U(z) = \alpha_2 z^{\beta_2}

\alpha_1 = 14, \beta_1 = 0.8 farmers
\alpha_2 = 10, \beta_2 = 0.4 recreation

. . .

  • Both benefits are increasing and concave, and both marginal benefits blow up at zero, so the answer is interior
  • First-order condition: F'(x) = U'(4 - x)

. . .

*The calibration is based on Miranda and Fackler, Chapter 7.

3 3. What the static model says and what it misses

3.1 Wrap it in a function

Code 
function solve_static(s)
    model = Model(Ipopt.Optimizer)
    set_silent(model)
    @variable(model, 0 <= x <= s, start = s / 2)
    @objective(model, Max, α_1 * x^β_1 + α_2 * (s - x)^β_2)
    optimize!(model)
    @assert is_solved_and_feasible(model)
    x_opt = value(x)
    return x_opt, s - x_opt, F(x_opt) + U(s - x_opt)
end
solve_static(4.0)
(3.721405661580687, 0.278594338419313, 46.05616209794752)

Same code from before, but now s is an argument and three numbers are returned: the irrigation, the recreation, and the total benefit

3.2 How does the optimum change with the reservoir level?

Blank 4. Preallocate three vectors, solve at each s, store the three results

Code 
function static_paths(s_grid)
    n = length(s_grid)
    x_path = zeros(n); z_path = zeros(n); W_path = zeros(n)
    for i in 1:n
        x_path[i], z_path[i], W_path[i] = solve_static(s_grid[i])
    end
    return x_path, z_path, W_path
end
s_grid = 1.0:0.5:30.0
x_path, z_path, W_path = static_paths(s_grid);

3.3 The static planner always drains the reservoir

Code 
share = 100 .* x_path ./ s_grid
plot(s_grid, share; xlabel = "s, water in the reservoir", ylabel = "% released for irrigation", label = false, ylims = (80, 100))

. . .

From 83% at one unit of water to 98% at a full reservoir. The more water there is, the more it goes to irrigation

3.4 Why?

Nothing in the objective values water tomorrow

. . .

\text{static:}\qquad F'(x) = U'(s - x)

. . .

\text{dynamic:}\qquad F'(x_t) = U'(s_t - x_t) + \color{#c22}{\delta \, \lambda_{t+1}}

. . .

\lambda_{t+1} is the value of one more unit of water in the reservoir next year. The static problem sets it to zero

3.5 How big is the missing term?

Next session we compute it. Here is the answer calculated at the level where the reservoir settles in the long run:

\lambda = \underbrace{0.975}_{\text{recreation now}} + \underbrace{8.775}_{\delta \, \lambda_{\text{next year}}} = 9.750

. . .

Ninety percent of what the water is worth is missing from today’s problem! That’s the reason the static planner drains the reservoir.

In the next two weeks, we’ll cover how to add it to the model and solve it
